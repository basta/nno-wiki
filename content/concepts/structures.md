---
title: Structures (model theory)
---

A **structure** on $\mathbb{R}$ — in the §7 sense used in this course — is a sequence

$$
\mathcal{R} = (\mathcal{R}_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}, \qquad \mathcal{R}_n \subseteq \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^n),
$$

specifying which subsets of each $\mathbb{R}^n$ are **definable**. The defining axioms (S1)–(S4) below pin down which collections deserve to be called structures; they are exactly what is needed for the class of definable sets to be closed under the natural set-theoretic and logical operations. Two further axioms (S5)–(S6) promote a structure to an [[concepts/o-minimal-structures|o-minimal expansion of the ordered real field]].

For NNO, the structure of interest is an [[concepts/o-minimal-structures|o-minimal expansion]] of the ordered real field.

## Axioms (S1)–(S4): structure

Each $\mathcal{R}_n$ is required to satisfy:

- **(S1) Boolean algebra.** $\mathcal{R}_n$ is a Boolean subalgebra of $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^n)$: closed under finite union, finite intersection, and complement, and contains $\emptyset$ and $\mathbb{R}^n$.
- **(S2) Cartesian product.** If $A \in \mathcal{R}_n$ and $B \in \mathcal{R}_m$, then $A \times B \in \mathcal{R}_{n+m}$.
- **(S3) Coordinate projection.** If $A \in \mathcal{R}_{n+1}$, then its projection $\pi(A) = \{(x_1, \dots, x_n) : \exists y\ (x_1, \dots, x_n, y) \in A\} \in \mathcal{R}_n$.
- **(S4) Diagonal.** The diagonal $\Delta = \{(x, x) : x \in \mathbb{R}\} \in \mathcal{R}_2$.

These four axioms are *exactly* what makes the class of definable sets match the class of sets cut out by first-order formulas (see below).

## Axioms (S5)–(S6): o-minimal expansion of the real field

To go from a generic structure to the setting used in NNO, two further axioms are imposed (see [[concepts/o-minimal-structures]]):

- **(S5) Contains the ordered real field.** $\{(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 : x < y\} \in \mathcal{R}_2$, and the graphs of $+ : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ and $\cdot : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ are in $\mathcal{R}_3$. Equivalently: $\mathcal{R} \supseteq \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{alg}}$.
- **(S6) o-minimality.** Every $A \in \mathcal{R}_1$ is a finite union of points and open intervals.

(S1)–(S4) alone are agnostic about which sets are definable — even $\mathcal{R}_\top$ satisfies them. (S5) pins down the ambient algebraic structure; (S6) is the *tameness* condition that propagates through (S3) to constrain every $\mathcal{R}_n$.

A **definable function** $f : A \to \mathbb{R}^m$ is one whose graph is definable — see [[concepts/definable-functions]].

## Poset of structures (Def. 7.8)

Structures on $\mathbb{R}$ form a poset under inclusion: $\mathcal{R} \le \mathcal{R}'$ iff $\mathcal{R}_n \subseteq \mathcal{R}'_n$ for every $n$. Two extremes:

- **Bottom $\mathcal{R}_\emptyset$.** The smallest structure: only "logically forced" sets (essentially diagonals, finite Boolean combinations of them, and their projections — very sparse).
- **Top $\mathcal{R}_\top$.** Every subset of every $\mathbb{R}^n$ is definable. Useless for tame geometry (no finiteness whatsoever), but it's a real element of the poset and a useful sanity-check endpoint.

The intersection of any family of structures is again a structure, so arbitrary meets exist. Arbitrary joins exist because the top structure does — so the poset is a complete lattice.

## Structure generated by a collection (Cor. 7.11)

Given a collection $\mathcal{G} \subseteq \bigcup_n \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ of "extra" sets we want to make definable, there is a **smallest structure containing $\mathcal{G}$**, denoted $\langle \mathcal{G} \rangle$ — obtained by intersecting all structures containing $\mathcal{G}$.

Concretely, $\langle \mathcal{G} \rangle$ is built by closing $\mathcal{G}$ under (S1)–(S4): take all Boolean combinations, products, projections, and reuses of the diagonal. This gives a presentation-by-generators picture: every structure is generated by some collection of "basic" definable sets.

## First-order formulas as a definability tool (Informal Thm. 7.32)

The (S1)–(S4) axioms are tuned so that the following equivalence holds.

> A set $A \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is definable in $\mathcal{R}$ iff there is a first-order formula $\varphi(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ in the language of $\mathcal{R}$ such that $A = \{(x_1, \dots, x_n) : \varphi(x_1, \dots, x_n)\}$.

The correspondence is mechanical:

| First-order formula | Set-theoretic operation |
|---|---|
| $\varphi \wedge \psi$ | intersection |
| $\varphi \vee \psi$ | union |
| $\neg \varphi$ | complement |
| $\exists y\ \varphi(x, y)$ | projection |
| $\forall y\ \varphi(x, y)$ | projection of complement |
| $x_i = x_j$ | use of the diagonal |

This gives two complementary proof strategies:

- **To show definable**: exhibit a first-order formula.
- **To show non-definable**: argue structurally about *any* would-be formula — typically by noting that first-order formulas have *fixed, finite quantifier depth* and cannot encode "$\exists n \in \mathbb{N}$" over an external $\mathbb{N}$.

The second strategy is what kills naive attempts to define [[concepts/binary-relations|transitive closure]] by an infinite union.

## Catalogue of structures (§7)

| Structure | Generated by | Captures | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathcal{R}_{\emptyset}$ | nothing | very little | smallest |
| $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{alg}}$ | polynomial sublevel sets | [[concepts/semialgebraic\|semialgebraic sets]] | o-minimal |
| $(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{alg}}, 2^{\mathbb{Z}})$ | $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{alg}} \cup \{2^{\mathbb{Z}}\}$ | semialgebraic + the discrete set $\{2^k : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ | **not** o-minimal — $2^{\mathbb{Z}}$ is an infinite discrete definable subset of $\mathbb{R}$, violating "finite union of points and intervals" |
| $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{PH}}$ | piecewise-homeomorphic / pathological generators | very wild | a typical "warning" structure — finiteness fails |
| $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{an}}$ | restricted [[concepts/real-analytic\|analytic]] functions | bump functions, $\exp$ on compact intervals | o-minimal |
| $\mathcal{R}_{\exp}$ | $\exp$ on $\mathbb{R}$ | sigmoid, softplus | o-minimal (Wilkie) |
| $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{an, exp}}$ | both | essentially all smooth activations | o-minimal |
| $\mathcal{R}_{\top}$ | everything | $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ for all $n$ | largest; useless |

The point of the catalogue is that o-minimality is a delicate property *between* the very poor $\mathcal{R}_{\emptyset}$ and the useless $\mathcal{R}_{\top}$, and adding seemingly innocent things (like the predicate $2^{\mathbb{Z}}$) can break it.
